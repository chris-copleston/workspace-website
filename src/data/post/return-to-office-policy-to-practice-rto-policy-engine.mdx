**A successful Return to Office strategy requires more than asking employees to come back. It requires clear expectations, transparent policies and the tools to make them work.**

The Return to Office (RtO) debate has moved beyond whether employees should work remotely or from the office. Organisations now face a more practical challenge: how to translate corporate attendance policies into consistent employee behaviour while making the office a productive, collaborative and worthwhile destination.

- For **HR leaders**, this means communicating expectations and monitoring compliance.
- For **Facilities Managers**, it means ensuring the right spaces are available when people need them.
- For **employees**, it means understanding when they are expected in the office, where they can work and how to plan their week.

Traditional desk and room booking systems help employees reserve spaces, but they rarely address the bigger question: **are employees actually complying with the company's workplace policy?**

## From Corporate Expectations to Everyday Accountability

Consider an organisation introducing a **three-day-per-week** office attendance policy.

With New Wave, administrators can configure weekly booking quotas, establish mandatory team days and determine which spaces employees are entitled to reserve.

Employees can see their booking entitlements and understand their attendance expectations.

If someone repeatedly reserves a desk but fails to attend, the Strike System provides a mechanism for warnings and, where company policy allows, reduced booking privileges.

The result is a more transparent relationship between employer expectations and employee responsibilities.

Importantly, the system can be configured to accommodate **approved leave, business travel and other exceptions**. A no-show is not automatically equivalent to an attendance-policy violation.
